**Proposed title:** What to include in a digital wedding invitation before you send it

**Editorial draft for review** (approximately 330 words)

> A digital wedding invitation works best when it answers the practical
> questions guests would otherwise send you one message at a time. Before you
> share a link, check the invitation from the perspective of someone who has
> never visited the venue and does not know the day's schedule.
>
> Start with the couple's names, the full date and the location. Write the month
> in words when your guests may use different date formats. If the ceremony
> and reception are in different places, label both clearly. Distinguish the
> arrival time from the ceremony start time so an early guest does not mistake
> one for the other.
>
> Make the venue easy to find. Include its full name, address and a map link
> that opens the correct entrance. A short note about parking or public
> transport can be more useful than a long description of the venue. For
> guests travelling from another country, state the local time zone.
>
> Explain the response you need. Include an RSVP deadline and make clear who
> is invited, whether partners or children are included, and how guests should
> communicate dietary or access requirements. Collect only information you
> need, and give guests a private way to discuss sensitive requests.
>
> Next, cover the details that affect preparation: dress guidance, outdoor
> surfaces, separate event schedules and any arrangements guests need to book
> themselves. Use direct, welcoming language. If a celebration has several
> events, make it clear which events each invitation covers.
>
> Finally, test the complete journey on a phone. Open the same link your guests
> will receive, check the text at a comfortable size, follow the map link and
> submit a test RSVP. Confirm where the host sees that response. Ask someone
> outside the planning group to try it and tell you what remains unclear.
>
> Keep one invitation link as the place for current information, and tell
> guests directly if an important detail changes. A clear invitation does more
> than look attractive: it helps people arrive prepared and reply with
> confidence.
